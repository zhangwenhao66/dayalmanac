## Site content used to ground the angle

Pulled from `独立站/dayalmanac/src/data/guides.ts`. DayAlmanac is very early-stage: only 7 published guides exist so far, all in one category ("Observances") — national-boyfriend-day, national-daughters-day, national-sons-day, national-coffee-day, national-grandparents-day, national-bosses-day, national-cat-day.

The real, defensible angle: the site's data schema treats "who started this observance" as a checkable claim rather than a decoration. Every guide has a typed `founding.status` field — `"documented"` (a named founder/proclamation/registration is on record) or `"unverified"` (no traceable origin, stated plainly instead of papered over). Of the 7 current guides, 3 are unverified (national-boyfriend-day, national-daughters-day, national-coffee-day) and 4 are documented. The clearest example: National Boyfriend Day has two incompatible origin stories circulating across the calendar-site industry — Dictionary.com dates it to 2012 (unnamed Twitter users), National Today dates it to 2014 — two years apart, never reconciled, and most calendar sites just pick one and present it as settled fact. DayAlmanac's page says outright that nobody can show who started it. This is a genuine methodology difference, not a marketing claim, and it's the kind of thing that reads as reference/data work rather than a promotional stunt — important given this niche's known perception risk.

## Outcome

0 of 9 candidates cleared both bars (real recent activity + real guest-interview slot fitting an independent calendar/reference-site operator). No pitches drafted, no humanizer/independent-review step needed, nothing sent.

## Why this niche was thin

The holidays/calendar/trivia podcast space splits into three buckets, none of which take outside pitches like this:
1. Vendor-owned shows run by competing calendar sites (National Day Calendar) — conflict of interest to pitch.
2. Fixed-panel banter/quiz/trivia formats (Hidden Holiday Hosts, Trivial Warfare, Trivia With Budds, No Such Thing As A Fish) — no guest-interview segment exists at all, so there's no pitch angle regardless of activity.
3. Shows that are topically adjacent but not actually about verifying "national day" claims (Let's Celebrate's pagan/ritual focus, general history podcasts) — real fit gap.

This mirrors the earlier beta finding (2 of 8 cleared the bar there) but is worse here: DayAlmanac's specific angle (origin-verification methodology) needs an interview-format show, and the shows that are topically closest to "holidays" turned out to be the ones least likely to have any outside-guest slot at all. Finding nothing that clears the bar is logged as an honest outcome per task instructions, not a shortfall to paper over.

Follow-up worth trying in a future pass: broaden to SEO/content-marketing/"niche site building" podcasts (which regularly interview independent site operators, resolving the guest-slot and title-bar problems) while still grounding any pitch in this same founding-status-verification angle — that reframes the guest as "how we built and fact-check a reference site" rather than "come talk about holidays," which may open real interview slots this batch didn't find.
